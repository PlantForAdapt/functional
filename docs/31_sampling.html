inventory <- readxl::read_excel("data/raw/inventory/inventory_Belterra_18062026.xlsx",

  select(PLACA, NOME_COMUM, NOME_CIENTIFICO, FAMILIA,

  mutate(botanical = str_extract(botanical, "^\\S+\\s+\\S+"),

  rowwise() %>% 
  mutate(dbh = max(CAP1, CAP2, CAP3, CAP4, CAP5, CAP6, CAP7)/pi) %>% 
  select(tree, x_utm, y_utm, 
         vernacular, botanical, family, bota_code, label, dbh) %>% 
  ungroup() # to remove row_wise

Objective

The functional traits will be used to:

  • characterize the ecological strategies of tree species in order to explain species demography and response to climate (T1.1)

  • predict the outcome of species interactions on tree performances under drough (T2.1)

For the first objectives, we can focus in the planted species, but for the second, covering as much as possible the diversity of all species (planted or naturally regenerating) is important. We will therefore aim to sample all the planted species, and non-planted species representing the highest proportion of the stem number that we can achieve.

We will sample on minimum of 5 trees per species and per sites to account for intraspecific variability. However, to minimise the effect of ontogentic trait variations, we will standardise this by sampling on the biggest individuals (as a proxy for the tallest). We traits to measure are listed on this page, on 5 leaves per individual, except for TLP for which we take only two leaves and Gmin. For wood density, we take two wood cores per individual.
